Every day the India Meteorological Department publishes a forecast meteogram for each of its ~1245 stations. We download each chart and read the cloud-cover panel pixel by pixel: the panel stacks high, middle, and low cloud, and how far the white fill rises in each band tells us the percent cover, sampled every three hours. We keep only the first forecast day and treat it as that day's reading, so a station's "cloudiness" is really the official model's forecast for the current day, not a satellite or sky measurement. On the map each station is a small tower of high, middle, and low cloud, and a place's overall cloudiness uses effective cover, the highest of the three layers at each moment, since from the ground the sky looks overcast if any layer is covering it. Reading numbers off a rendered chart is approximate by nature, so the pipeline validates chart geometry and flags bad extractions, but treat everything here as an illustrative forecast rather than a precise measurement.
